# Your ActiveState narrative

This is the language to use when the departure comes up, with recruiters and in
interviews. It is built from your record, not from a template. Three beats:
what happened, where you were standing, what you know now.

## The narrative

"ActiveState restructured around AI. About half the company went, roughly
twenty people, every function. Mine was one of them.

I was on the building side of it. I spent the year putting Claude into product
discovery, requirements and competitive research, and before ActiveState I ran
the first AI-driven task automation for the analyst team at Malwarebytes. So I
watched that part of the work compress up close.

What I took from it is pretty specific. AI moved on research and documentation
faster than I expected it to. It has not moved on working out what a customer
actually needs, or on calling what to build when the data is incomplete. That
is where I spend my time."

## Short version, for a recruiter screen

A recruiter screen is six minutes and there is no room to develop the full
answer. Give them this and stop.

"ActiveState restructured around AI and cut about half the company, mine
included. I had spent the year building the AI workflows into the product
process, so I was on the building side of it."

## When they ask why you

"Half the company, about twenty people, every function. It was structural. The
company was rebuilt at a different size."

## When they ask why not here

This is the one that decides the interview. Do not get defensive, and do not
argue that AI is worse than it is.

"It took the research and the drafting. It did not take the customer
conversations, and it did not take the calls you make when the data is
incomplete. I would rather work somewhere that is clear about where that line
sits, and I can help find it, because I have already had to."

## On delivery

You have something most product candidates do not. You deployed this twice, at
two different companies, and you have the receipts for both. You are not
someone it happened to. Stay on that side of the line when you tell it.

Do not learn any of this word for word. Hold the three beats and let the words
come out however they come out that day. Anything delivered verbatim sounds
delivered, and that is the one thing that would undo it.

Do not grade the decision as wrong, even by tone, and skip any joke about it.
Both read as someone still working through it.

Use me as a reference. I will describe it the same way you do: restructuring
around AI, about half the company, every function.

If any of this does not sound like you, tell me and we will change it. It has
to be yours or it will not hold under a follow-up question.
