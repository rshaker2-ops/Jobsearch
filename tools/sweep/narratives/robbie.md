# Your ActiveState narrative

This is the language to use when the departure comes up, with recruiters and in
interviews. It is built from your record, not from a template. Three beats:
what happened, where you were standing, what you know now.

## The narrative

"ActiveState restructured around AI. About half the company, twenty people or
so, every function. My role was one of them.

I was in the security function while that was happening, and I built some of
the automation myself. The threat intelligence pipeline, the news feed tooling,
and a lot of the vendor risk workflow that moved onto Vanta. So I saw exactly
where the manual load came off.

It came off evidence gathering and questionnaire routing, and it came off fast.
What stayed is the assessment itself. Judging whether a vendor's answer is
actually good enough, and sitting across from an auditor. I have run 95 vendor
assessments and 30 customer questionnaires. That is the part I am good at."

## Short version, for a recruiter screen

A recruiter screen is six minutes and there is no room to develop the full
answer. Give them this and stop.

"ActiveState restructured around AI and cut about half the company. My role was
one of them. I was in the security team through it and built some of the
automation myself."

## When they ask why you

"Half the company, about twenty people, every function. It was not about
individuals. The company was rebuilt at a different size."

## When they ask why not here

This is the one that decides the interview. Do not get defensive, and do not
argue that AI is worse than it is.

"It took the evidence gathering and the routing. It did not take the judgment.
Somebody still has to decide whether a vendor's answer is acceptable, and
somebody still has to sit with the auditor. I would rather be the person who
knows which is which."

## On delivery

Keep it short. Analyst screens move fast, and a long answer to this question
reads as anxious no matter how good the words are. Stop after "that is the part
I am good at" and let them take it from there.

Do not learn any of this word for word. Hold the three beats and let the words
come out however they come out that day. Anything delivered verbatim sounds
delivered, and that is the one thing that would undo it.

Do not grade the decision as wrong, even by tone, and skip any joke about it.
Both read as someone still working through it.

Use me as a reference. When you give my name, say I was your CPTO at
ActiveState in the same breath. We share a surname and you want that to arrive
as context rather than as something a recruiter works out on their own.

If any of this does not sound like you, tell me and we will change it. It has
to be yours or it will not hold under a follow-up question.
